Why Guild.ai?

I have spent the last six months building the customer-side version of what Guild is shipping. At ESC Partners I owned Maya AI from vision to launch in 2.5 months: an autonomous, event-driven agent platform on Oracle CCS that runs across voice, chat, SMS, email, and mobile for a 200K-resident utility. The agent acts on its own when confident; when it is not, a supervisor sees the draft in Microsoft Teams and decides with one click. That supervisor cockpit is the local version of a control plane (identity, audit, takeover, observability) built for one customer at a time.

Guild is the same problem at platform scale. Every enterprise that wants to put agents into production needs the centralized identity, least-privilege access, immutable audit log, and cost visibility you are building. The Agent Hub also speaks to me because the agentic workflows I have shipped at ESC (Maya AI plus the Conference Outreach pipeline) are exactly the kind of builds the Hub is meant to host: open, builder-first, governed by default. The role description reads like the next logical step after Maya AI.

The funding context matters too. The $44M Series A from Khosla, GV, NFX, and Acrew gives Guild the runway to define this category properly. I want to be the first PM in that room.

Tell me about a time you took something from zero to one.

Maya AI is the cleanest example. ESC had no AI track when I joined. The CRO wanted Oracle CCS to be smarter, faster, and multi-channel. There was no design doc, no eval framework, no team.

I started with two weeks of customer discovery at NEP. Sat with supervisors. Watched them dig through Oracle CCS to answer a single resident question. Mapped the workflow. Found the failure points: language barriers, response time, manual data entry, no escalation path.

I wrote a one-page product narrative. Picked the stack: AWS Bedrock for model routing, n8n for workflow orchestration, FastAPI for the integration layer, React for the supervisor cockpit, Microsoft Teams adaptive cards for the human-in-the-loop approval, 11 Labs for the 26-language voice surface.

I built it on Claude Code, end-to-end, in 2.5 months. Single AI hire. Maya AI is now live at NEP with 200K+ residents, rolling out at Moreno Valley Electrical, piloting at Delta Utilities. It handles 70 to 80% of first-touch support autonomously and removes 100+ manager emails per day. Projected savings at NEP are around $225K per month.

Founder mentality is not a phrase from a posting for me. It is how I worked when I was bootstrapping CloudApproach. It is how I work now.
